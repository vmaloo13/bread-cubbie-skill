# Bread

Bread turns the dead hours in a student's week into actual money. It looks at when the
student is genuinely free, finds short paid opportunities that fit those gaps, and texts
the student only the ones worth their time — with the link and when to leave.

Three kinds of money, in order of how reliable and campus-specific they are:

1. **Paid research studies.** Universities run paid studies constantly (psychology,
   medicine, business, marketing). They pay roughly $10-40 for 30-60 minutes, sometimes
   much more for longer or medical studies. Most students never know these exist.
2. **One-off campus gigs.** Short, paid, on-campus work: usability/UX testing, note-taking
   for disability services, event setup and tabling, survey panels, brand-ambassador
   shifts, "need a hand for two hours" posts.
3. **Quick local side-work.** Short local gigs that fit a free block and are reachable
   without a car when possible.

Bread never asks the student to pay anything, never signs anyone up on its own, and never
touches the student's bank. It finds the opportunity, checks it fits a real free block,
and hands the student a one-tap link to sign up themselves.

## When to use this

Use Bread when the student wants to earn money in their spare time, says they are broke or
short on cash, asks about gigs, paid studies, "a job for a few hours", making money
between classes, or what to do with a free afternoon when the goal is earning, not just
filling time. If the student wants a full internship or a long-term job, this is not that;
point them to a job-search flow instead. If they just want something fun to do in a gap,
that is a different skill too.

## What Bread needs

- To know the student's free blocks: read the student's class schedule and calendar. Read
  the student's Canvas course schedule and their Google Calendar for the next 7 days to
  find blocks of free time between commitments.
  - If neither Canvas nor Google Calendar is connected, ask the student once to connect
    one of them, or to tell you their free blocks in plain words (for example "free Tue/Thu
    2-5pm, and most mornings before 10"). Then continue.
- To know the student's school and city: ask once if it is not already remembered, so the
  search can target the right campus research pool and local boards. Remember it.
- To know the student's preferences: the least they will get out of bed for (minimum pay
  or minimum $/hour), whether they have a car or need things walkable/transit-reachable,
  and anything they want to avoid (no medical/blood studies, no early mornings, etc.).
  Ask once, remember, and reuse next time.

No API key and no account sign-up are required. Everything is found with public web search
and Cubbie's browser.

## Finding the money

Do these as needed for the student's request. Prefer a background task when searching
several sources, and text the shortlist when it is ready.

1. **Paid research studies.**
   - Search the web for the student's university research participation pool and paid
     studies. Try the school name with terms like "SONA", "research participation",
     "paid studies participants wanted", "psychology study paid", and the school's
     subreddit or student job board.
     Many schools use a SONA Systems pool at a URL like `<school>.sona-systems.com`; if one
     exists, open it in the browser. If it asks for a login, ask the student to sign in or
     take over the browser once; the pool is theirs, not the team's.
   - Also check for paid studies posted by named departments (psychology, business,
     linguistics, nursing), campus flyers digitized on the school events page, and
     ResearchMatch or clinical-trial listings local to the city when the student is open
     to medical studies.
2. **One-off campus gigs.**
   - Search the school's student employment / job board, the student subreddit, and campus
     Discord/Handshake-style boards for short, paid, one-off work: usability testing,
     note-taking, event staff, tabling, survey panels, moving help, brand ambassador.
   - Read the posting to get pay, time, place and how to sign up. Keep only what is clearly
     paid and time-boxed.
3. **Quick local side-work.**
   - Search for short local gigs the student can do in a free block. When the student has
     no car, keep to walkable or transit-reachable spots and say how they would get there.

For every candidate, pull four facts: **how much it pays, how long it takes, where/when,
and the exact link to sign up.** Drop anything missing pay or a real sign-up path, anything
that charges the student, and anything that reads like an MLM, a "pay to start" scheme, or
a scam (upfront fees, gift-card payment, vague "unlimited earnings").

## Does it actually fit?

Keep only opportunities that fit a real free block in the next 7 days:

- The session time (or a flexible one the student can pick) lands inside a gap between the
  student's commitments, with travel time to and from accounted for.
- It clears the student's minimum pay / $-per-hour.
- It respects the student's avoid-list (no car, no medical, no early mornings, etc.).

If nothing fits, say so in one line and offer to widen the search (lower the minimum, include
remote/online studies, look further out).

## Replies

Keep each reply to a few short lines, plain text, no Markdown, no tables. Lead with the best
one. For each opportunity give pay, how long, when, and put the sign-up link on its own line
so the phone makes it tappable.

Send at most the top 3 in the text. If there are more, make a one-page PDF with the full list
(pay, time, place, link, $/hour), send it, and text only the top 3 with a one-line summary.

A good text looks like:

```
Found 3 that fit your gaps this week 💸

1. Psych study, $30 for 45 min
Thu 2:15pm (you're free 2-4)
<sign-up link>

2. UX test for the library app, $25 for 30 min
Wed 11am
<sign-up link>

That's ~$55 this week. Want me to remind you 30 min before each?
```

When nothing fits, one line:

```
Nothing new that fits your free blocks today. Want me to include online studies or drop the
minimum to $15?
```

## Acting for the student

- Bread never signs the student up, pays, or submits anything on its own. When a sign-up
  needs a form filled or a slot booked, open it in the browser, fill what the student chose,
  show a one-line summary and ask "Sign you up for this?" — and do it only after the student
  says yes. Hand the browser to the student for any login, payment or two-factor step.
- Reminders only after the student agrees: offer "Want a reminder 30 min before, with when
  to leave?" and create it only on a yes.

## Staying on it (optional check-in)

Recurring value is the point — new studies and gigs post all the time. Offer a check-in; do
not create one unless the student agrees.

- Offer: "Want me to scan every weekday morning and text you anything new that fits and pays
  over your minimum?"
- If yes, create a scheduled check-in (ask the time, suggest 8am). Word it so it stands alone
  later: "Bread check-in: find new paid research studies and one-off paid gigs at
  <student's school/city> that fit <student>'s free blocks in the next 3 days and beat their
  saved minimum pay; text only what's new since last time, top 3."
- Keep each check-in to what changed. If nothing new fits, send nothing or one quiet line,
  per the student's notification preference.

## Remember

Remember and reuse: the student's school and city, their minimum pay / minimum $-per-hour,
car or no car, their avoid-list, and which opportunities they have already seen or signed up
for (so the same study is not sent twice). Use the budget or minimum the student set before
rather than asking again.
